A Grammar Lesson from the Time Traveler's Logbook

Will vs. Would
The Time Traveler's Rule

Every sentence about the future sends you down one of two paths: the path that will definitely happen, or a path that would only happen if something else were true first.

A real plan or promise A maybe, an "if," a guess

The Rule

Which path is your sentence on?

WILL

Use it for something that is really, definitely going to happen — a firm plan, a promise, a fact about the future.

"I will finish my homework before dinner."

WOULD

Use it for something that depends on a condition, or that isn't real — an "if," a wish, a guess, or something from the past imagining the future.

"If I had more time, I would finish my homework."

From the Logbook

Real Mix-Ups, Fixed

These are actual sentences where a writer used "would" for something that was really going to happen.

I would use my allowance to pay for a new storybook.
→ I will use my allowance to pay for a new storybook.

This is a firm promise, not a hypothetical — so it needs "will."

I hope you would accept me in this programme.
→ I hope you will accept me in this programme.

A real hope about a real future outcome takes "will," not "would."

If I had more time, I would finish my homework.

This one's correct — it starts with "if," describing something uncertain, so "would" is right.

Beyond the Basics

Four Special Cases

The quiz below includes a few trickier patterns that aren't just "firm plan" or "if." Here's what to look out for.

WILL

Scheduled Facts

A timetable or fixed schedule is treated like a certain fact, even though no one "promised" it.

"The bus will leave at exactly 8 o'clock."

WOULD

Reported Speech

When you report what someone said in the past, "will" shifts back to "would" — even if their plan was firm at the time.

He said he would call me back later.

WOULD

"If I Were You..."

This classic advice phrase imagines being in someone else's shoes — it's always hypothetical, so it always takes "would."

"If I were you, I would apologise first."

WOULD

Polite Wishes

"Would love to" or "would like to" express a wish or desire, not a firm plan — even when you really mean it.

"I would love to visit Japan one day."
