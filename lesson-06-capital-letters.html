Who Gets the VIP Pass?

Think of a capital letter as a VIP pass. Every word in a sentence would love one, but you're the guard at the door, and passes go only to two kinds of guest: the first word of a sentence, and a special name that belongs to one particular person, place, day or thing. Everyone else comes in through the normal entrance, in small letters.

Your turn at the door

Tap each word that deserves a VIP pass (a capital letter). Tap again to take it back. Then check your guest list.

The door check

Ask these two questions about any word. If the answer to either one is yes, the word gets a capital letter.

1

Is it the first word of a sentence? That includes the first word of a sentence inside speech marks.

Last week, my mum called out, "Dinner is ready!"

2

Is it the special name of one particular person, place, day, event or group?

We met Ms Goh at Parkway Parade on Saturday.

The guard's test: could you swap the word for a general word, like "a mall", "a teacher" or "a country"? If so, it's a special name, and it gets a pass. The general word stays small.

Parkway Parade → a mall. Ms Goh → a teacher. Japan → a country.

And one word always gets a pass, wherever it stands: I.

Three traps at the gate

The first two are opposites: forgetting a pass, and handing one out when it isn't needed. The third catches words in the middle of a sentence.

Trap 1: Forgetting the pass for a name

I visited singapore in december and saw the esplanade.

I visited Singapore in December and saw the Esplanade.

Countries, months and buildings are all special names. Watch for words made from a name too: Singapore → Singaporeans, Japan → Japanese, England → English.

Trap 2: Name or just a description?

My Mum and I went to the Community Club near our School.

My mum and I went to the community club near our school.

Thank you, Mum! / Mum and I went to Casuarina Community Club.

After my, our, the or a, words like mum, grandmother, teacher and school are just descriptions, so they stay small. Use a capital only when the word is used as a name, or when it's part of the full name.

Trap 3: A pass in the middle of a sentence

After signing the consent form, What must I do?

After signing the consent form, what must I do?

"We are back!" My parents exclaimed.

"We are back!" my parents exclaimed.

A comma doesn't start a new sentence, so the next word stays small. After speech, the speech tag ("my parents exclaimed") still belongs to the same sentence, so it stays small too.

Who's on the VIP list

Special names come in four main groups. If a name has more than one word, every main word gets a pass.

People
  • names
  • Mr / Mrs / Ms / Mdm / Dr
  • Mum, Dad (as names)
  • I

Nurul and I helped Mdm Rahimah carry the books.

"Can I go to the park, Dad?" asked Marcus.

Places
  • countries
  • cities
  • roads
  • buildings and malls
  • parks

We drove along Orchard Road to Gardens by the Bay.

My uncle works at Changi Airport in Singapore.

Days and events
  • days
  • months
  • festivals
  • named events

Racial Harmony Day falls on a Friday this July.

We visit my grandparents during Chinese New Year and Hari Raya Puasa.

Groups and languages
  • languages
  • nationalities
  • class names
  • clubs and schools

Our English teacher speaks Japanese too.

Arjun from Primary 5F asked a Primary Six student for help.

Special guests

The quiz checks these too. They follow their own small rules.

"The" stays small

Some names come with "the" in front. In the middle of a sentence, "the" stays small. Only the name itself gets the pass.

We took photos with the Merlion and watched a show at the Esplanade.

Seasons stay small

Spring, summer, autumn and winter are not special names, so they stay small. Months, though, always get a pass.

We flew to Korea in Winter.

We flew to Korea in winter, in December.

Titles of books, songs and shows

The first word and every main word get a pass. Small words like a, an, the, of, in, on, and stay small, unless they come first.

The Secret Of The Old Kampong

The Secret of the Old Kampong

Opening and closing a letter

In "Dear Sir/Madam," both titles get a pass. In the closing, only the first word gets one.

yours faithfully, / Yours Sincerely,

Yours faithfully, / Yours sincerely,

Pin This Up

  • Pass 1: the first word of every sentence, including the first word inside speech marks.
  • Pass 2: special names of people, places, days, months, festivals, events, languages, nationalities and classes.
  • Always: the word "I".
  • No pass: general words (a mall, my mum, our school), seasons, a word after a comma, and a speech tag after a quote.
  • Titles: capitalise the main words. Letters: "Yours sincerely" has only one capital.
