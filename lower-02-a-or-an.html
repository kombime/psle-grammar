Meet Helper N

Before a naming word, we say a or an.

Most words just use a. But when the next word starts with an open-mouth sound, little N jumps in to help: an!

Tap a picture. Does N come to help?

Two steps

Do this before every naming word.

1

Say the next word out loud.

2

Does it start with one of these open-mouth sounds?

aeiou

Yes → an   No → a

Look at the very next word

Sometimes a describing word comes first. Then that word decides.

an red apple → a red apple

a old bus → an old bus

"Red" starts with an r sound, so it's a. "Old" starts with an o sound, so it's an.

Which group?

Say each one out loud. Can you hear the first sound?

an

Open-mouth sound first

  • 🍎an apple
  • 🥚an egg
  • 🐜an insect
  • 🍊an orange
  • ☂️an umbrella

a

Any other sound first

  • ⚽a ball
  • 🐱a cat
  • 🐟a fish
  • ✏️a pencil
  • 🐰a rabbit

Two tricky ones

Listen to the sound, not the letter.

🦄 A "u" that says "you"

Some words start with u but sound like "you". They use a.

a unicorn  ·  a uniform

But: an umbrella (an "uh" sound).

⏰ An "h" you can't hear

In "hour", the h is silent. It sounds like "our", so it uses an.

an hour

But: a hat, a horse (you can hear the h).
