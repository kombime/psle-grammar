A Grammar Lesson · Punctuating Speech

Inside the
Speech Bubble!

Picture quotation marks as a speech bubble in a comic. Anything that's literally part of the spoken words — including the punctuation mark right at the end — stays inside the bubble.

The Method

The 3-Step Check

1

Open the bubble. If a speech tag comes before the quote, put a comma right before the opening quotation mark. She said, "I'm sorry."

2

Look at what's being said. A statement gets a full stop (.), a question gets a question mark (?), an exclamation gets an exclamation mark (!).

3

Look at what comes AFTER the closing quote. Does the sentence stop here, or does more follow, like a speech tag? This is the step most people get wrong!

WATCH OUT!

A full stop never sits right before a speech tag that continues the sentence — even though the quoted words look like a finished sentence.

"Yes." my mother said.

"Yes," my mother said.

But a question or exclamation mark never changes into a comma: "Where is mother?" I asked.

The Four Cases

Where Does the Punctuation Go?

A
Tag BEFORE the quote

Always put a comma right before the quote opens — no matter what kind of sentence follows inside.

She said, "I'm sorry."

Ms Goh asked, "Who would like to volunteer?"

B
Tag AFTER + a statement

The trickiest case. Swap the full stop for a comma, because the sentence isn't over — the tag still has to finish it.

"I am sorry I dropped it," I said guiltily.

"We should leave now," she whispered.

C
Tag AFTER + a question/exclamation

Unlike statements, ? and ! never change into a comma — they stay exactly as they are.

"Where is mother?" I asked.

"Watch out!" he shouted.

D
No speech tag at all

Punctuate the sentence inside the bubble normally — statement gets a full stop, question gets a question mark.

"I am going to the toilet."

"How do I sign up?"

Beyond the Basics

Three More Things to Watch

The quiz below includes a few finer details that aren't about comma-vs-full-stop. Here's what to look out for.

1

Two Sentences, One Quote

If a character's speech contains two separate complete ideas, each one still needs its own full stop — don't join them with a comma, even though they're inside the same quotation marks.

"She is on her way, the restaurant is crowded today."
"She is on her way. The restaurant is crowded today."

2

Space After the Quote

Always leave a space between the closing quotation mark and whatever comes next — a missing space is a easy-to-miss but common slip.

"Congratulations!"the coach yelled.
"Congratulations!" the coach yelled.

3

Capital to Start the Quote

When a new sentence of speech begins inside quotation marks, it starts with a capital letter — just like any other sentence would.

My mum called out, "dinner is ready."
My mum called out, "Dinner is ready."
