Only One Piece Fits

Prepositions are the small joining pieces of a sentence: in, on, at, to, of, into, through. They're like jigsaw pieces. Lots of them look almost the same, but in each gap, only one has the right shape. Sometimes the shape comes from a place: is it inside something, or on top of it? Other times it comes from the word right before the gap. For example, "interested" only ever clicks with "in".

Finding the right piece

When you reach a gap, ask these three questions in order.

QUESTION 1

Is it about a place, or movement? Picture the space. Is it inside something, on a surface, at one spot, or moving into something?

We sat on the balcony. The ball rolled into the drain.

QUESTION 2

Is it a fixed pair? Some words always take the same partner. Don't guess from the meaning. Learn the pair as one chunk.

interested in, reply to, in shock, a grip on

QUESTION 3

Is a piece missing? Read the sentence aloud. Some words can't connect to the next word without their piece.

Please reply me. → Please reply to me.

ininside a space
onon a surface
atone spot
intomoving inside
ontomoving on top
throughin one side, out the other

Three traps

Every example here comes from a real composition.

Trap 1: A missing piece

I hope you will reply me soon. → I hope you will reply to me soon.

I poured a glass water. → I poured a glass of water.

Meet me at the shop downstairs our block. → Meet me at the shop at the foot of our block.

If the sentence sounds like it's skipping a step, a piece is probably missing.

Trap 2: Guessing the partner from the meaning

I am interested to join. → I am interested in joining.

She covered her mouth with shock. → She covered her mouth in shock.

I lost grip of the iPad. → I lost my grip on the iPad.

"Into" sounds right for joining something, and "with" sounds right for a feeling. But fixed pairs don't follow logic. They're chunks you have to know.

Trap 3: Inside or on top?

We were playing games in the balcony. → We were playing games on the balcony.

I walked into the stage. → I walked onto the stage.

A balcony, a stage and a field are surfaces you stand on, not boxes you're in. If you walk "into" a stage, you crash into it!

The four boxes of pieces

Two boxes hold place pieces, and two hold fixed pairs.

Where it is

in a space or area: in the canteen, in the school hall, in Singapore.

on a surface, floor or vehicle: on the balcony, on the stage, on the bus, on the MRT.

at one spot: at the bus stop, at the door, at the foot of our block.

Where it's going

into: The ball rolled into the drain.

onto: I walked onto the stage.

through: I peeked through the peephole.

out of: Students ran out of their classrooms.

Verb + piece

Raj apologised to Mary for spoiling her book.

I would like to enrol in the programme.

  • reply to
  • listen to
  • apologise to (a person)
  • apologise for (a thing)
  • enrol in
  • take part in
  • lend a hand to
  • depend on
Describing word or phrase + piece

Ms Goh was proud of us. Lila stared at me in disbelief.

Out of the corner of my eye, I saw Mum.

  • interested in
  • good at
  • proud of
  • afraid of
  • in shock
  • in disbelief
  • a grip on
  • a glass of
  • out of the corner of my eye

Three more puzzles in the quiz

These follow their own small rules.

Time pieces: at, on, in

ata clock time: at 7 a.m., at noon, at recess
ona day or date: on Saturday, on 22 August, on Youth Day
ina longer stretch: in December, in 2026, in the morning

Think of it as getting bigger: a point (at), then a day (on), then a month or more (in).

After a piece, a verb takes -ing

When a verb comes straight after a preposition, it takes -ing. This catches lots of letters to teachers.

I look forward to hear from you. → I look forward to hearing from you.

Are you interested in sign up? → Are you interested in signing up?

Sometimes no piece at all

Some words connect directly, with no piece in between. Adding one is just as wrong as leaving one out.

We discussed about the plan. → We discussed the plan.

We entered into the hall. → We entered the hall.

I went to home. → I went home.

Pin This Up

  • Place: use in for a space, on for a surface or vehicle, and at for one spot.
  • Movement: use into, onto, through or out of.
  • Fixed pairs: reply to, interested in, enrol in, a grip on, in shock, proud of. Learn them as chunks.
  • Time: at 7 a.m., on Saturday, in December.
  • After a preposition, a verb takes -ing: look forward to hearing.
  • No piece: discuss the plan, enter the hall, go home.
