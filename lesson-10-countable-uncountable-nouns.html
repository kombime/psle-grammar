Count It or Scoop It?

Picture the mama shop near your block. Some things sit on the counter one by one: sweets, eggs, erasers. You count them: "three erasers, please." Other things sit in big jars or sacks: rice, sugar, ice. You can't count grains of rice, so you scoop them and count the bags instead: "a bag of rice." English nouns work the same way. Some go on the counter tray, and some go in the scoop jar, even ones you can't touch, like advice and homework.

Tray or jar?

Every noun goes in one place, and that decides which words can go with it.

Counter tray: countable

You can put a number in front: one egg, two eggs.

  • one: a / an egg
  • more than one: add -s, eggs
  • many, a few, fewer
Scoop jar: uncountable

You measure it, pour it, or it's an idea. No number fits: two rices.

  • no a / an: a rice
  • no -s: rices
  • much, a little, less
  • singular verb: the rice is

The shop test: say "two ___" or "three ___" out loud.

"Three chairs" works, so chair goes on the tray. "Three furnitures" sounds wrong, so furniture goes in the jar. To count a jar word, count its container or its pieces: three pieces of furniture, a bag of rice.

Three traps

The first one turned up again and again in real compositions.

Trap 1: Adding -s to a jar word

further clarifications → further clarification

all my homeworks → all my homework · informations → information · equipments → equipment

These words feel like "lots of things", so it's tempting to add -s. But they're scoop-jar words, and jar words never take -s.

Trap 2: Putting "a" or "an" on a jar word

Grandpa gave me an advice. → Grandpa gave me some advice / a piece of advice.

I heard a news. → I heard some news / a piece of news.

"A" means "one", and you can't have one of a jar word. Use some, or count it with a piece of.

Trap 3: Using the wrong amount word

How many money do you have? → How much money do you have?

Less people came because of the rain. → Fewer people came because of the rain.

Many, few, fewer are tray words. Much, little, less are jar words. Check which place the noun belongs in first.

What's in the jars, and how to count them

Learn these groups of jar words, the measuring words that count them, and which amount words go with each place.

Jar 1: things you pour, scoop or weigh
  • water
  • milk
  • rice
  • sugar
  • bread
  • ice
  • sand
  • paper
  • glass
  • plastic
  • money

The water in the pool was cold. Mum bought some rice and bread.

Jar 2: "lots of things" words
  • homework
  • furniture
  • luggage
  • equipment
  • stationery
  • information
  • advice
  • feedback
  • news
  • rubbish
  • traffic
  • scenery
  • weather

Thank you for your advice and feedback. All the furniture was new.

These are the sneaky ones. They describe a whole group, so the group word stays single. The countable parts are different words: pens and rulers are stationery; chairs and tables are furniture.

Jar 3: feelings and ideas
  • fun
  • help
  • support
  • patience
  • courage
  • knowledge
  • excitement

We had lots of fun. Thank you for all your help and support.

Measuring words: how to count a jar word

Measuring word + of + jar word. The measuring word is countable, so it takes a/an and -s.

  • a glass of water
  • a bowl of rice
  • a loaf of bread
  • a slice of cake
  • a sheet of paper
  • a carton of milk
  • a spoonful of sugar
  • a piece of advice / news / information / furniture / luggage
  • a pair of shoes / scissors

I drank two glasses of water. The coach loaded six pieces of luggage.

Don't drop the "of": a glass water → a glass of water.

Four tricky shelves

The quiz checks these too.

Jar words take a singular verb

A jar word is one mass, so it takes is / was / has, even when it looks plural.

The news is good. The equipment was checked. Mathematics is my favourite subject.

News and mathematics end in -s, but they're single jar words.

Same word, two shelves

Some words change place when their meaning changes.

paper: the material. Wrap it in colourful paper.
a paper: a test or newspaper. Two exam papers.
time: minutes passing. Not enough time.
a time: an occasion. Three times. We had a great time.
glass: the material. Made of glass.
a glass / glasses: a cup, or spectacles.
chicken: the food. Roast chicken.
a chicken: the bird. Ten chickens.
hair: all of it. Long black hair.
a hair: one strand. A hair in my soup!
room: space. Is there room for one more?
a room: a place with walls. Two rooms.
artwork: art as an activity. We did artwork.
artworks: separate pieces in a gallery.

Already plural: no -s needed

People, children and police are already plural. Don't add -s, and use a plural verb.

many peoples → many people · The children were excited. The police have arrived.

Groups of a number

After "groups of" or "teams of", the number stays a plain number.

We were split into groups of threes.

We were split into groups of three.

Pin This Up

  • Tray (countable): a/an, -s, many, a few, fewer, a number of.
  • Jar (uncountable): no a/an, no -s, much, a little, less, an amount of, singular verb.
  • Sneaky jar words: homework, furniture, luggage, equipment, stationery, information, advice, feedback, news.
  • To count a jar word: a piece of advice, a glass of water, a loaf of bread.
  • Watch for: words with two meanings (paper, time), already-plural people/children, and "groups of three".

Count it on the tray, with many and -s;
scoop it from the jar: much, and no -s!
