Every Story Is a Photo Album

When you write a story, you're showing your reader photos of something that has already happened. Every caption describes a moment that's over, so it's written in the past. If one caption slips into the present, it's like a live video suddenly playing in the middle of your album. The reader is jolted out of the story.

Tap each box to switch the verb. Make every caption fit a story that already happened, then develop the photo.

The album check

Run these three checks on every verb in a story.

1. Is it a caption?

If you're telling what happened in the story, use the past. That includes was, could, would and had to.

Ms Goh explained that each class needed twelve dancers. My hand shot up.

2. Is it an older photo?

If something happened even earlier than the moment you're describing, use had + done. It's a photo from further back in the album.

When I reached the bus stop, the bus had already left.

3. Is it a recording?

Words inside speech marks are a voice recording, kept exactly as they were spoken. They can be in the present. The caption around them stays past.

"I am so nervous!" I whispered.

Three traps

All three come straight from real compositions.

Trap 1: A live video in the album

Ms Goh told us that Youth Day was coming. Each class needs twelve dancers.

Ms Goh told us that Youth Day was coming. Each class needed twelve dancers.

We searched everywhere, but we can't find the keys.

We searched everywhere, but we couldn't find the keys.

Watch the little verbs most: is → was, are → were, can → could, will → would, needs → needed. They slip into the present without you noticing.

Trap 2: Using "had" for the next photo

I noticed mould on the marshmallows. I had checked the other packets too.

I noticed mould on the marshmallows. I then checked the other packets too.

"Had + done" is only for something earlier. Checking the packets came after noticing the mould. That's the next photo, so it takes the simple past.

Trap 3: The wrong form after a helper word

I had did it! → I had done it!

We managed to spent time with our cousins. → We managed to spend time with our cousins.

The helper word already shows the past. After had, use the "done" form. After to, did, could, would, use the plain verb.

What goes on each page

There are four kinds of verb in a story, and each has its own job.

Past captions

This is the main story, one moment after another.

The bell rang, and we rushed to the hall.

While I was waiting in the queue, Arjun tapped my shoulder.

I could not believe my eyes. It was my lost wallet!

Older photos

Use had + done for something earlier than the moment you're describing. Look out for these signal words:

  • realised that
  • remembered that
  • by the time
  • already
  • never … before
  • because (an earlier cause)

When I reached home, I realised that I had left my wallet on the bus.

I had never seen so many people in one place before.

Recordings

Inside speech marks, keep the words exactly as they were said, even if they're in the present.

"I am going to the toilet," I said.

"What should I do?" I wondered.

"I can't find my book!" Nurul cried.

Helper words

The helper word sets the form of the verb that comes after it.

had +done, gone, seen, eaten, taken
to +plain verb: managed to spend
did not +plain verb: did not apologise
could / would +plain verb: could see

Special pages

The quiz checks these too. They come up a lot in PSLE stories.

Reported speech: no speech marks, no recording

When you tell what someone said without speech marks, it becomes a caption. Each verb steps one page back.

"Each class needs twelve dancers." → Ms Goh said that each class needed twelve dancers.

"I will call you." → He said he would call me.

"I did not do it!" → Levin said that he had not done it.

"If" in a past story

When your character imagines something in a past story, use the past after "if" and would in the result.

If I stand up to the bully, I would get hurt.

If I stood up to the bully, I would get hurt.

If I wanted to play, I would have to skip the queue.

The lesson at the end stays present

Your final reflection is a note written on the album's last page today. A lesson that's always true stays in the present, even though "I learnt" is past.

I learnt that honesty is always the best policy.

That day, I learnt that we must never give up.

Fix the album

Every question is part of a story told in the past. Some ask you to fill a blank, some to check a sentence, and some to pick the best version. The questions get trickier as you go.
